# CLINICAL PRACTICE GUIDELINE: CARDIOVASCULAR TRIAGE AND HYPERTENSIVE MANAGEMENT

## SECTION 1: ACUTE CHEST PAIN TRIAGE AND EMERGENCY RED FLAGS
Acute chest pain or pressure requires immediate triage to rule out Acute Coronary Syndrome (ACS), aortic dissection, pulmonary embolism, or tension pneumothorax.
- Red Flag Symptoms: Crushing substernal chest discomfort, pain radiating to left arm, neck, or jaw, accompanied by acute dyspnea, diaphoresis, lightheadedness, or sudden syncope.
- Triage Protocol: Any presentation matching these red flags must be classified as an IMMEDIATE EMERGENCY. The patient must call emergency services (911/112) or be transported to the nearest Emergency Department. Do not delay emergency transport for outpatient evaluation.

## SECTION 2: HYPERTENSION DIAGNOSTIC CRITERIA AND FIRST-LINE THERAPY
Hypertension is defined as persistent elevated blood pressure:
- Normal Blood Pressure: Systolic < 120 mmHg AND Diastolic < 80 mmHg.
- Stage 1 Hypertension: Systolic 130-139 mmHg OR Diastolic 80-89 mmHg.
- Stage 2 Hypertension: Systolic >= 140 mmHg OR Diastolic >= 90 mmHg.
- Hypertensive Crisis: Systolic > 180 mmHg AND/OR Diastolic > 120 mmHg.

First-line Pharmacotherapy for Essential Hypertension:
1. ACE Inhibitors (e.g., Lisinopril 10-40 mg daily) or ARBs (e.g., Losartan 50-100 mg daily). Preferred in diabetic patients and those with chronic kidney disease (CKD).
2. Thiazide Diuretics (e.g., Chlorthalidone or Hydrochlorothiazide 12.5-25 mg daily).
3. Calcium Channel Blockers (e.g., Amlodipine 5-10 mg daily).

## SECTION 3: HEART FAILURE AND FLUID OVERLOAD WARNING SIGNS
Patients with underlying heart failure (HFrEF or HFpEF) require monitoring for decompensation:
- Signs of Acute Decompensation: Rapid weight gain (> 2-3 lbs in 24 hours or 5 lbs in a week), progressive dyspnea on exertion, orthopnea (difficulty breathing lying flat), paroxysmal nocturnal dyspnea, and severe bilateral lower extremity pitting edema.
