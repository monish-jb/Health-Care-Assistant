# CLINICAL PRACTICE GUIDELINE: ENDOCRINOLOGY, THYROID FUNCTION, AND DIABETES MELLITUS

## SECTION 1: EVALUATION OF THYROID DISORDERS (HYPOTHYROIDISM AND HYPERTHYROIDISM)
Thyroid-Stimulating Hormone (TSH) is the primary screening analyte for thyroid dysfunction.
- Reference Normal TSH Range: 0.45 to 4.50 mIU/L.
- Primary Hypothyroidism: Elevated serum TSH (> 4.50 mIU/L) with decreased serum Free T4 (< 0.8 ng/dL).
  - Common Symptoms: Generalized chronic fatigue, cold intolerance, unexplained weight gain, constipation, dry skin, muscle weakness, and memory impairment.
  - Standard Treatment: Oral Levothyroxine (typically 1.6 mcg/kg daily starting dose). Taken on an empty stomach 30-60 minutes before breakfast.
- Primary Hyperthyroidism: Suppressed serum TSH (< 0.10 mIU/L) with elevated Free T4 or Free T3.
  - Common Symptoms: Palpitations, heat intolerance, tremor, unintended weight loss, frequent bowel movements, anxiety, and insomnia.

## SECTION 2: DIABETES MELLITUS DIAGNOSIS AND GLYCEMIC TARGETS
Diagnostic Thresholds for Type 2 Diabetes Mellitus:
1. Glycated Hemoglobin (HbA1c): >= 6.5%. (Prediabetes: 5.7% - 6.4%).
2. Fasting Plasma Glucose (FPG): >= 126 mg/dL (7.0 mmol/L) after 8-hour fast.
3. 2-Hour Oral Glucose Tolerance Test (OGTT): >= 200 mg/dL (11.1 mmol/L).
4. Random Plasma Glucose: >= 200 mg/dL with classic hyperglycemia symptoms (polyuria, polydipsia, unexplained weight loss).

First-Line Pharmacotherapy for Type 2 Diabetes:
- Metformin: Initial dosage 500 mg daily with meals, titrated up to 1000 mg twice daily. Metformin improves insulin sensitivity and inhibits hepatic gluconeogenesis.
- Contraindications: Severe renal impairment (eGFR < 30 mL/min/1.73m2) due to lactic acidosis risk.
