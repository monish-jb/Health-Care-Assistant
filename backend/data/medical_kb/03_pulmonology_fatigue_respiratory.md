# CLINICAL PRACTICE GUIDELINE: CHRONIC FATIGUE PROTOCOL AND RESPIRATORY DISORDERS

## SECTION 1: SYSTEMATIC APPROACH TO ADULT CHRONIC FATIGUE
Fatigue lasting longer than 2 weeks requires structured clinical inquiry rather than generic symptom suppression.
- Key History Questions Needed:
  1. Duration and onset (acute onset vs gradual onset over months).
  2. Severity and impact on daily functioning.
  3. Associated B-symptoms: Night sweats, unexplained weight loss (>5-10% body weight), or low-grade fever.
  4. Sleep hygiene and daytime drowsiness (evaluating for obstructive sleep apnea).
  5. Current medications (antihistamines, beta-blockers, sedatives).

- Essential Laboratory Workup for Persistent Fatigue:
  - Complete Blood Count (CBC) with differential (rule out anemia or occult infection).
  - Serum TSH and Free T4 (rule out hypothyroidism).
  - Fasting Plasma Glucose / HbA1c (rule out early diabetes).
  - Comprehensive Metabolic Panel (CMP - renal function, liver enzymes, electrolytes).
  - Vitamin D (25-hydroxyvitamin D) and Vitamin B12 levels.

## SECTION 2: ACUTE COUGH AND RESPIRATORY INFECTIONS
- Acute Bronchitis: Cough lasting 1-3 weeks. Majority (>90%) are viral. Routine antibiotic administration is NOT recommended.
- Community-Acquired Pneumonia (CAP) Red Flags: High fever (>102°F), purulent rusty sputum, pleuritic chest pain, tachypnea (>24 breaths/min), and pulse oximetry SpO2 < 92% on room air. Requires prompt chest radiography and targeted antibiotic coverage (e.g., Amoxicillin or Azithromycin).
