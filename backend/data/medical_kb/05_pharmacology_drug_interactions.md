# CLINICAL PRACTICE GUIDELINE: PHARMACOLOGY, DRUG INTERACTIONS, AND SAFETY

## SECTION 1: COMMON DRUG INTERACTIONS AND CONTRAINDICATIONS
- NSAIDs (e.g., Ibuprofen, Naproxen) + Antihistamines / Antihypertensives:
  - NSAIDs can attenuate the antihypertensive effects of ACE inhibitors, ARBs, and loop diuretics.
  - Concurrent use of NSAIDs with ACE inhibitors and diuretics increases the risk of acute kidney injury ("Triple Whammy" effect).
  - Patients taking Warfarin, Aspirin, or Direct Oral Anticoagulants (DOACs) should avoid NSAIDs due to severe gastrointestinal bleeding risk.

- Statin Therapy (e.g., Atorvastatin, Simvastatin) + Fibrates or Azole Antifungals:
  - Concomitant administration increases the risk of severe myopathy and rhabdomyolysis.

- Acetaminophen (Paracetamol) Safety Limits:
  - Maximum daily adult dose: 3000 mg - 4000 mg per 24 hours from all sources.
  - Exceeding maximum limits carries acute hepatotoxicity and hepatic necrosis risk. Avoid alcohol concurrent consumption.

## SECTION 2: MEDICATION ADHERENCE AND SIDE EFFECT COUNSELING
- ACE Inhibitors (Lisinopril): Common side effect includes a dry persistent cough (due to bradykinin accumulation). If intolerable, switch to an ARB (Losartan). Rare severe adverse effect includes angioedema (swelling of face/lips/tongue), which requires immediate emergency care.
- Metformin: Common side effect includes mild gastrointestinal upset (diarrhea, nausea). Risk is minimized by taking with meals and gradual dose titration.
