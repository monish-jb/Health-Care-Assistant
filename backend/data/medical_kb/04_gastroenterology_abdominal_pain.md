# CLINICAL PRACTICE GUIDELINE: GASTROENTEROLOGY AND ABDOMINAL PAIN DIAGNOSTICS

## SECTION 1: ABDOMINAL PAIN QUADRANT LOCALIZATION AND TRIAGE
Abdominal pain evaluation depends heavily on pain location, acuity, and accompanying signs:
- Right Upper Quadrant (RUQ) Pain: Differential includes acute cholecystitis, biliary colic, hepatitis. Look for positive Murphy's sign, fever, and jaundice.
- Right Lower Quadrant (RLQ) Pain: Differential includes acute appendicitis. Look for McBurney's point tenderness, localized rebound tenderness, fever, and leukocytosis. Acute appendicitis requires urgent surgical evaluation.
- Left Lower Quadrant (LLQ) Pain: Differential includes acute diverticulitis. Often presents with fever and change in bowel habits in older adults.
- Epigastric Pain: Differential includes GERD, peptic ulcer disease (PUD), and acute pancreatitis (radiating to the back, associated with elevated serum lipase).

## SECTION 2: GASTROESOPHAGEAL REFLUX DISEASE (GERD) AND ALARM SYMPTOMS
- GERD Management: First-line dietary modification (avoiding late-night meals, caffeine, fatty foods) plus Proton Pump Inhibitor (PPI) trial (e.g., Omeprazole 20 mg daily 30 min before breakfast for 4-8 weeks).
- GERD Alarm Symptoms (Requiring Immediate Endoscopy): Dysphagia (difficulty swallowing), odynophagia (painful swallowing), persistent vomiting, hematemesis (blood in vomit), melena (black tarry stool), or significant unintended weight loss.
